## Introduction

Theology—the disciplined reflection on the nature of the divine, religious claims, and ultimate reality—is one of the oldest intellectual traditions in human history. Whether approached from personal conviction, historical curiosity, or philosophical inquiry, studying theology equips you to understand the ideas, controversies, and textual traditions that have fundamentally shaped Western culture, political theory, moral philosophy, and literature. This curriculum approaches theology through a rigorous academic and ecumenical lens, emphasizing critical reading, historical context, and conceptual clarity.

This curriculum is intended for self-directed beginners with no prior formal background in religious studies, ancient languages, or philosophy. It is equally suited for secular learners seeking to understand the intellectual architecture of religious thought and confessional learners who want an academically rigorous foundation beyond basic devotional reading. The only prerequisite is a willingness to read complex texts closely and engage unfamiliar worldviews with analytical charity.

### How to Move Through the Material

The subjects in this curriculum are arranged in a intentional pedagogical sequence, moving from foundational vocabulary to historical context, doctrinal synthesis, and philosophical critique:

* **Start with Method:** Begin with **Introduction to Theological Studies** to learn how theological questions are formulated and acquire the essential vocabulary used across the discipline.
* **Establish the Texts and History:** Move to **Biblical Foundations** to understand the literary genres, historical contexts, and theological themes of the Hebrew Bible and New Testament. Follow this directly with the **History of Christian Thought**, which traces how communities interpreted those texts to forge creedal orthodoxy from antiquity through the Reformation.
* **Synthesize and Interrogate:** With historical and biblical foundations in place, study **Systematic Theology** to examine how doctrines (such as Christology, the Trinity, and soteriology) fit together into coherent conceptual systems. Finally, engage **Philosophy of Religion** to examine the metaphysical coherence and epistemological warrants of these claims using the tools of analytic philosophy.

This page contains only the core foundational curriculum: the biblical, historical, systematic, and philosophical knowledge that every student of theology must master before specializing. It intentionally avoids advanced electives, ancient language acquisition, pastoral ministry skills, and narrow denominational polemics. Once you have completed this foundational sequence, you can explore specialized subfields—such as patristics, advanced hermeneutics, and analytic apologetics—in [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md).

## Curriculum

### Introduction to Theological Studies

Explores the nature, scope, and method of theology, establishing the vocabulary and conceptual frameworks needed to study religious belief systematically.

[Theology: The Basics (Alister E. McGrath)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781119064220) - A concise, accessible primer that introduces foundational theological terms, major historical debates, and the core articles of Christian confession.

[Faith Seeking Understanding: An Introduction to Christian Theology (Daniel L. Migliore)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780802882851) - A comprehensive alternative to McGrath's primer that explores theological method, divine revelation, and biblical authority while engaging classical traditions in contemporary dialogue.

### Biblical Foundations (Old and New Testament)

Provides a historical, literary, and critical survey of the primary scriptural texts that form the foundation of Western theological thought.

[Introduction to the Old Testament (Hebrew Bible) (Yale Open Courses)](https://oyc.yale.edu/religious-studies/rlst-145) - A comprehensive, historically grounded 24-lecture course by Christine Hayes examining the literature, history, and religious thought of ancient Israel.

[Introduction to the New Testament History and Literature (Yale Open Courses)](https://oyc.yale.edu/religious-studies/rlst-152) - Designed to follow Hayes's course directly, Dale B. Martin's 26-lecture series analyzes the origins of early Christianity and the historical-literary context of the New Testament canon.

### History of Christian Thought

Traces the development of theological doctrines, key controversies, and major thinkers from the early church through the Reformation and into the modern era.

[The Story of Christianity, Volumes 1 and 2 (Justo L. González)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780061855887) - The standard two-volume narrative textbook tracing the evolution of Christian thought, institutions, and major figures from the early church to the contemporary global era.

[A Journey through Western Christianity: from Persecuted Faith to Global Religion (200 - 1650) (Yale University / Coursera)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/western-christianity-200-1650) - A free-to-audit online university course taught by Bruce Gordon that pairs with the textbook to examine the theological conflicts, monastic movements, and reformations that shaped Western thought.

### Systematic Theology

Organizes and synthesizes biblical, historical, and philosophical data into a coherent framework of core doctrines, such as the nature of God, Christology, and soteriology.

[Christian Theology: An Introduction (Alister E. McGrath)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781118869574) - The canonical undergraduate textbook that systematically maps out the historical development and doctrinal definitions of Christian dogmatics.

[The Christian Theology Reader (Alister E. McGrath)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781118874387) - The direct primary-source companion to McGrath's textbook, curated to be read alongside each doctrinal chapter to engage the original writings of historical theologians.

### Philosophy of Religion

Applies rational inquiry and philosophical methods to examine the metaphysical and epistemological foundations of religious belief, including arguments for the existence of God and the problem of evil.

[Philosophy of Religion: An Anthology (Louis P. Pojman and Michael Rea)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781111526924) - A comprehensive primary-source collection of classical and contemporary philosophical essays addressing theism, evil, religious epistemology, and miracles.

[Philosophy of Religion (Matt McCormick / California State University, Sacramento)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLUJ-vlKYyqzAaiZJLOZyyXuHTfi1SS_6y) - A full 31-lecture university series that works directly through the major analytical arguments, proofs, and counterarguments anthologized in Pojman and Rea.

[A History of Philosophy (Arthur Holmes / Wheaton College)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL9GwT4_YRZdBf9nIUHs0zjrnUVl-KBNSM) - An optional 81-lecture survey from ancient Greece to modern thought that provides the broader philosophical background necessary for advanced theological vocabulary.
